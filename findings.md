Phase 1: Base Model & Dataset Preparation
The pipeline begins with a shared foundational model, but the data requirements for SFT and RL split immediately.

The Base Model: The researchers used Qwen 2.5 3B-Instruct for both the math and science tasks.

The Datasets:
Math: Open-Reasoner-Zero dataset.
Science Q&A: The Chemistry L-3 subset of the SciKnowEval dataset.
Data Processing (The SFT Bottleneck): RL only requires the initial prompt and a binary success indicator (the correct final answer). However, SFT requires complete, step-by-step reasoning trajectories.
Teacher Distillation: Because the raw datasets lacked full reasoning chains, the researchers queried stronger models to generate them. They used DeepSeek R1 for Math and GPT-4o for Science, filtering out any responses where the generated final answer didn't match the ground truth.

Phase 2: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) PipelineThis is the offline baseline where the model learns to mimic the teacher's reasoning.Objective: The model is trained using a standard cross-entropy loss against the external annotations (the teacher-generated reasoning chains).Implementation Details:Precision: bfloat16.Optimizer: AdamW.Hyperparameter Sweeps: Learning rates from 1e-5 to 9e-5, batch sizes from 16 to 128, and 1 to 2 epochs.

Phase 3: Reinforcement Learning (GRPO) PipelineThis is where the paper's core discovery happens. The researchers used Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), an on-policy RL algorithm that utilizes negative examples without needing a separate critic model.The Reward Function: A strict, sparse binary reward. The model receives a 1 if its final extracted answer matches the ground truth, and a 0 if it does not.The "Secret Sauce": Notably, the researchers did not use an explicit KL penalty in the loss function during RL training. The preservation of prior knowledge happened organically because RL samples from its own distribution (on-policy), intrinsically anchoring it near the base model.Implementation Details:Group Setup: 8 prompts per generation, with a group size of 64.Hyperparameter Sweeps: Learning rates from 1e-5 to 5e-5 for 1 epoch.

Phase 4: Evaluation and the Pareto FrontierTo properly measure catastrophic forgetting, you cannot just look at a single training run. You must evaluate the trade-off between new task acquisition and old task retention across the entire hyperparameter sweep.New Task Metric: Calculate raw accuracy on the held-out Math or Science test sets.Prior Task Metric (Forgetting): Evaluate the fine-tuned models on a diverse suite of general benchmarks: Hellaswag, TruthfulQA, MMLU, IFEval, Winogrande, and HumanEval.The KL Predictor: Calculate the Forward KL divergence ($\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\tau}[KL(\pi_{0}||\pi)]$) between the fine-tuned model and the base model on the new task.Analysis: Plot the Pareto frontier. You will find that while SFT improves on the new task by drastically altering its internal representations (high KL shift), RL achieves the exact same new-task accuracy with minimal KL shift, thereby preserving its prior knowledge.

Run a sweep of learning rates (e.g., 1e-5 to 9e-5), batch sizes (16 to 128), and train for 1 or 2 epochs.
